# v0.4.0 evaluation report

Date: 2026-09-04

This release was evaluated on ZCode, not Codex. The Codex account used for v0.3.0 was
out of quota, and the runner was made host-pluggable rather than tied to one vendor.
Configuration: `zcode-cli 0.16.5`, model `builtin:bigmodel-coding-plan/GLM-5.2`,
`effort=max thinking=enabled(32000)`, four workers, two trials per case, 300s timeout.
Every run used a fresh temporary `HOME` and workspace; the provider config was
referenced by symlink and never copied or read.

Raw evidence:

- [`v0.4.0-behavior.json`](../eval-results/v0.4.0-behavior.json)
- [`v0.4.0-trigger.json`](../eval-results/v0.4.0-trigger.json)

## Headline result

**v0.4.0 is not distinguishable from v0.3.0 on this task distribution.** Both Skill
arms beat the no-Skill baseline by a clear margin. The release is justified by the
gaps it closes and by the absence of regression, not by a measured quality gain.

## Behavior comparison

Twenty-seven cases ran against no Skill, the previous release restored from git, and
this release. All 162 runs completed. A separate blind call graded each complete
case/trial group; all 54 grades succeeded, 8 of them after one recorded retry.

| Arm | Mean grade (0-4) | Graded pass | Wins | Mean seconds | Input tokens/run | Output tokens/run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `agent-harness-design` | 3.796 | 98.1% | 21 | 42.9 | 37,342 | 2,513 |
| `agent-harness-design-v030` | 3.685 | 100% | 17 | 40.4 | 35,882 | 2,345 |
| `no_skill` | 3.296 | 98.1% | 4 | 38.0 | 18,246 | 1,946 |

Twelve of 54 comparisons were ties.

## Paired differences with uncertainty

Arms answer identical cases, so differences are paired. Mean paired difference in
grade, with standard error and a normal 95% interval:

| Comparison | n | Mean difference | SE | 95% interval | Better / equal / worse |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| v0.4.0 − v0.3.0 | 54 | +0.111 | 0.086 | [−0.058, +0.280] | 14 / 32 / 8 |
| v0.4.0 − no Skill | 54 | +0.500 | 0.108 | [+0.288, +0.712] | 34 / 13 / 7 |
| v0.3.0 − no Skill | 54 | +0.389 | 0.085 | [+0.222, +0.556] | 25 / 25 / 4 |

The interval for v0.4.0 against v0.3.0 crosses zero. The win count of 21 to 17 does
not survive this check and must not be quoted as an improvement. Both Skill arms
improve on no Skill with intervals well clear of zero.

Score distribution:

| Arm | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `agent-harness-design` | 1 | 9 | 44 |
| `agent-harness-design-v030` | 0 | 17 | 37 |
| `no_skill` | 1 | 36 | 17 |

v0.4.0 produces more top scores than v0.3.0 (44 against 37) and one failing score.

## The one regression signal

v0.4.0 scored 2 on `ordinary_task_does_not_activate`, a case that checks the Skill
does **not** turn an ordinary request into a harness exercise. v0.3.0 scored no case
below 3. One observation is not a trend, but the direction is the one this release
should watch: the expanded material may make over-engagement on ordinary work more
likely. Re-check this case first on the next iteration.

Cost also moved in the expected direction: v0.4.0 uses about 4% more input tokens than
v0.3.0 and roughly twice the no-Skill baseline, for 2.5s more latency per run.

## Implicit discovery

Twelve cases ran twice with marker-only probe Skills for this Skill,
`task-state-with-files`, and `agent-delegation`, installed at `.zcode/skills` inside a
fresh home. All 24 runs completed and all 24 made the accepted selection.

| Route class | Exact accepted selections |
|---|---:|
| Positive harness requests | 10/10 |
| Negative non-harness requests | 6/6 |
| Ambiguous | 2/2 |
| Adjacent-Skill coexistence | 6/6 |

**This measures ZCode's implicit discovery and is not comparable to the v0.3.0 figure
of 20/24, which measured Codex.** A different host selects Skills differently; nothing
here shows that the Skill's own triggering improved. Coexistence behaved correctly in
both directions: the delegation-plus-audit case loaded both Skills, the pure delegation
case loaded only `agent-delegation`, and the long-task case loaded only
`task-state-with-files`.

## Deviations from the v0.3.0 methodology

Stated plainly, because none of them is neutral:

1. **Different host and model.** v0.3.0 ran on Codex CLI with `gpt-5.4-mini` at low
   reasoning. This ran on ZCode with GLM-5.2 at maximum effort. Arms are matched within
   this run, so the comparisons above hold; the numbers are not comparable to the
   v0.3.0 report.
2. **Different baseline arm.** v0.3.0 compared against the legacy `agents-best-practices`
   Skill. That snapshot is not reproducible on this machine — three candidate copies
   were checked and none matched the recorded digest `e57f5640…` — so the previous
   release of this Skill is the baseline instead, restored from commit `562408e` and
   verified to digest `234fb865…`.
3. **Different structured-output mechanism.** Codex grading used `--output-schema`.
   ZCode 0.16.5 has no equivalent, so the grader receives the schema in its prompt and
   the output is validated on return, recorded as `prompt_json_validated`. Invalid
   grader output is retried with a fresh real call; 8 of 54 grades needed one retry.
   The grader remains blind: responses are labeled A/B/C with an order rotated per case
   from a hash of case name and trial.
4. **Host surfaces present in every arm.** A fresh ZCode home receives the bundled
   official plugins, and MCP servers from the linked provider config connect on every
   run. These are identical across arms but mean the environment is not bare.
5. **Run boundedness is wall-clock only.** ZCode 0.16.5 documents `--max-turns` but
   rejects it at parse time, so the 300s timeout is the only bound.

An earlier behavior artifact from the same day was discarded rather than reported. It
was produced before three later changes to the runner, one of which rewrote the grading
prompt, so its grades did not come from the grader being shipped. Its headline was a
wider 25-to-10 win margin; the run reported here, produced by the committed runner,
narrows that to a difference that is not statistically distinguishable. Reporting the
discarded numbers would have overstated the change.

## Launch decision

Ship v0.4.0. It closes four documented gaps in the guidance, adds two references, and
corrects a misquoted citation, without regressing the accepted task distribution and
while remaining clearly ahead of no Skill. It does **not** demonstrate a quality gain
over v0.3.0 at this sample size, and the report should not be summarized as if it did.

Signals that would trigger another architecture review: repeated sub-3 scores on
activation-negative cases, a widening cost gap without a matching quality gain, or a
host where implicit discovery of this Skill falls below the adjacent-Skill baseline.
